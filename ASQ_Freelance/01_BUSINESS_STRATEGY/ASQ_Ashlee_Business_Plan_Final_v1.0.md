## 1. Executive Summary

ASQ Ashlee designs client-controlled AI operations systems for independent businesses. The system connects a company’s business information, repeatable workflows, and approval rules inside a Google-based operating environment, then gives the owner a conversational interface through Google Chat.

The central thesis is simple:

> The most valuable AI layer for a small business is not a chatbot or a collection of prompts. It is an interpretable business context and controlled execution system that can turn ordinary-language requests into reliable, reviewable work.

Arts of August, a Maine coastal art studio operated by artist Kaleigh Anderson, is the first complete reference implementation. Its existing system already spans artwork records, product configurations, listings, website content, events, opportunities, marketing workflows, image handling, and human approval rules. The Google migration will test whether that founder-led system can become a secure, measurable, and repeatable client installation.

ASQ Ashlee is seeking to validate four things before claiming commercial scale:

1. The Google-based system can execute useful workflows reliably while retaining human control.
2. A meaningful portion of the architecture can be reused across clients.
3. The system produces measurable time savings, revenue enablement, or operational consistency.
4. Independent businesses will pay enough to support implementation, maintenance, and founder labor.

A potential $15,000 friends-and-family validation round would fund dedicated founder implementation time, development equipment, legal and business foundations, software and cloud testing, and two external pilot deployments. The appropriate financing instrument has not yet been selected and would require legal and tax guidance.

## 3. The Problem

Independent creative businesses often operate across disconnected tools and undocumented human memory. A single new product may require the owner to:

- organize files and images;
- create inventory or catalog records;
- write descriptions and accessibility text;
- configure products and variations;
- update a website;
- create marketplace listings;
- plan social and email promotion;
- record events or sales opportunities; and
- answer inquiries.

The problem is not merely that these tasks take time. It is that the business repeatedly loses context as work moves between tools. Owners must remember what happened, re-enter the same information, and re-explain their preferences to different systems.

Many existing software products solve one functional slice of the business but add another dashboard, subscription, data boundary, or learning burden. General-purpose AI assistants can generate text, but they usually lack the durable business records, permissions, workflow state, and approval rules required to perform operational work safely.

### Problem hypothesis to validate

The target customer experiences enough recurring administrative friction that a conversational, context-aware operations system creates measurable value and willingness to pay.

### Claims not yet validated

ASQ Ashlee has not yet established a defensible average of “hours saved per week” across customers. This will be measured during the Arts of August migration and external pilots rather than asserted in advance.

ASQ Ashlee installs a client-controlled conversational operations layer using Google Workspace and Google Cloud.

The intended client experience is:

1. The owner describes a change or uploads a source asset.
2. The system retrieves the appropriate business context and rules.
3. A bounded workflow prepares a proposed action.
4. The owner reviews the result in Google Chat.
5. The owner approves, edits, or rejects it.
6. The system performs the authorized action and records what happened.

### Working category

**Conversational business operations infrastructure for independent creative businesses.**

## 7. Why Interpretable Context Methodology

ASQ Ashlee uses Interpretable Context Methodology, or ICM, as its chosen systems foundation.

ICM was developed by Jake Van Clief and David McDermott and introduced in their research paper, *Interpretable Context Methodology: Folder Structure as Agentic Architecture*. The methodology proposes that understandable folder structures, plain-language context files, staged workflows, and human review can provide a practical alternative to heavier agent-orchestration frameworks for many sequential forms of work.

ASQ Ashlee did not originate ICM.

The founder’s role is to select, apply, test, and extend the methodology inside real independent-business operations—beginning with Arts of August.

### Why ASQ uses ICM

Reliable AI systems require more than access to a capable model. A model can generate, summarize, classify, and reason, but it cannot independently know which business source is authoritative, which relationships must be preserved, what the client considers correct, what information may be inferred, what requires human judgment, which tools may be used, or whether a workflow was completed successfully.

ICM offers a practical structure for making those decisions visible and maintainable.

Rather than hiding orchestration inside an opaque automation framework, the system can represent stages, responsibilities, source material, context, and handoffs through understandable workspace structure.

### Context before automation

Many AI implementations begin with a task: write this listing, respond to this email, update this website, or create a marketing post.

An ICM-informed system begins one layer earlier:

- What information should govern the task?
- Which source is authoritative?
- What context must be loaded?
- What work belongs to AI?
- What work should remain deterministic?
- What may not be inferred?
- Where must a human review the result?
- What file or record carries the approved output to the next stage?

A capable model operating on incomplete or conflicting context can produce polished but unreliable work.

### Human-readable agentic architecture

ICM is aligned with ASQ because it keeps the operating architecture legible.

Numbered stages, bounded folders, markdown instructions, source records, decision logs, and approval handoffs can be inspected by both the human operator and the AI system.

This supports systems that are easier to understand, debug, review, transfer, and adapt across models.

### The durable business asset

Models, vendors, and interfaces will continue to change. A business’s underlying context is more durable: its products, services, history, relationships, brand language, operating rules, decisions, workflows, source assets, and human judgment.

Using ICM, ASQ aims to preserve that context outside any single chat, prompt, model, or application.

The client’s organized business context—not the chatbot—becomes the central operational asset.

### ASQ’s application of ICM

ASQ is applying ICM to a broader small-business operating environment that includes:

- source-authority rules;
- structured business records;
- relationships among artwork, products, events, content, and opportunities;
- staged workflows;
- explicit approval boundaries;
- provenance and decision history;
- deterministic scripts for mechanical work;
- AI assistance where interpretation or generation is useful;
- human-readable documentation; and
- evaluation of whether each workflow achieved its intended result.

### Research foundation and ASQ contribution

Jake Van Clief and David McDermott provide the methodological foundation.

ASQ Ashlee’s contribution is the real-world implementation layer:

- adapting ICM to independent-business operations;
- integrating it with Google Workspace and Google Cloud;
- designing client-facing approval experiences;
- creating reusable business schemas and workflow patterns;
- testing reliability, usability, and economics;
- documenting where the methodology works well;
- identifying where additional software or orchestration is required; and
- developing a repeatable implementation practice for future clients.

ASQ should therefore describe itself as an **ICM-informed systems company**, not the creator or owner of ICM.

### The ICM thesis

> ASQ believes ICM provides a strong foundation for building understandable, human-controlled AI systems for independent businesses.

Google supplies the infrastructure. Gemini and other models provide reasoning and generation capabilities. ICM supplies the interpretable workspace structure through which context, workflow stages, deterministic tools, and human judgment can be coordinated.

## 8. Model Roles and AI Governance

ASQ Ashlee uses a role-based AI strategy rather than relying on one model for every type of work.

The methodology is selected and governed by the founder. ICM provides the structural foundation for organizing context, workflow stages, handoffs, source material, and human review. AI models operate within that structure; they do not define it.

### Gemini: system execution

Gemini is the intended primary execution model for the Google-based Arts of August system.

Its role may include interpreting approved business context, analyzing artwork images, drafting structured metadata, preparing workflow outputs, interacting with Google Workspace and Google Cloud services, supporting Google Chat approval experiences, and executing bounded steps after human authorization.

Gemini is selected because it sits closest to the proposed Google infrastructure and may offer practical advantages in integration, latency, administration, and usage-based cost.

This remains subject to proof-of-concept testing. ASQ will measure actual quality, reliability, latency, and cost.

### ChatGPT: thinking partner and co-strategist

ChatGPT serves as ASQ Ashlee’s primary thinking partner and strategic review layer.

Its role includes challenging assumptions, identifying missing questions, separating evidence from founder conviction, testing business and product logic, synthesizing work across systems, developing positioning and language, reviewing plans for overstatement, maintaining continuity across the broader ASQ strategy, and translating complex ideas into understandable business decisions.

ChatGPT is not positioned as the autonomous operator of client systems. Its primary value within ASQ is strategic reasoning, synthesis, critique, and decision support.

The founder remains responsible for final business, product, and ethical decisions.

### Claude: independent code review and technical check

Claude is currently used as a secondary technical reviewer for code and implementation work produced with Gemini or other coding tools.

Its role may include reviewing code for errors and missing cases, questioning architectural choices, identifying security or maintainability risks, checking whether implementation matches written requirements, comparing code behavior with the intended workflow, suggesting tests, and providing an independent model perspective before deployment.

Claude does not replace formal testing, security review, or human technical judgment.

### Separation of responsibilities

ASQ does not allow model output alone to establish business truth or authorize high-impact actions.

The operating hierarchy is:

1. The founder and client define goals, rules, permissions, and authoritative sources.
2. ICM structures the context and workflow.
3. Gemini or another approved execution model performs bounded operational work.
4. ChatGPT supports strategic analysis, system reasoning, and review.
5. Claude or another secondary model may review code or technical implementation.
6. Deterministic tests validate mechanical behavior where possible.
7. A human approves consequential actions.

This reduces the risk of allowing one model to generate, review, approve, and execute its own work without meaningful independent checks.

### Model flexibility

The current model assignments reflect the tools available during validation. They are not permanent architectural requirements.

ASQ intends to preserve portable context, documented workflows, model-independent business records, replaceable model interfaces, clear evaluation criteria, and the ability to change models when cost, capability, safety, or client needs justify it.

The system should remain governed by the methodology and business context—not by loyalty to a particular model provider.

### The founder’s role

The founder’s contribution includes selecting ICM as the operating methodology, assigning roles to different AI systems, deciding which model is trusted for which class of work, designing review relationships among models and humans, evaluating whether outputs meet real business needs, and retaining final accountability for system design and client outcomes.

The models contribute capability. The founder defines the system in which that capability is used.

- usable from the client’s actual phone;

A single successful demonstration is not sufficient.

ASQ is not attempting to replace every specialized platform.

Traditional systems offer mature functionality but can require multiple dashboards, subscriptions, configuration layers, and ongoing manual context transfer.

ASQ’s proposed advantage is a conversational layer that coordinates work across the client’s existing environment while preserving understandable context and approval rules.

### Compared with general AI assistants

General assistants can help think and write. ASQ’s proposed system adds:

- durable business records;
- workflow state;
- controlled tool access;
- client-specific context;
- approvals;
- logs; and
- operational continuity.

### Compared with no-code and AI app builders

Tools that generate applications can accelerate interface or code creation. ASQ’s value is intended to reside in the business architecture, context design, operating rules, implementation, and stewardship—not code generation alone.

### Competitive limitation

Google and other large platforms may increasingly package similar capabilities. ASQ must therefore compete through domain knowledge, implementation quality, trust, usability, and repeatable outcomes rather than assuming technical exclusivity.

## 19. Risks and Mitigations

### Technical reliability

**Risk:** Model or integration failures could create incorrect actions.  
**Mitigation:** Bounded workflows, human approval, validation rules, logging, retry limits, and recovery paths.

### Client data and permissions

**Risk:** Misconfigured access could expose or damage client information.  
**Mitigation:** Per-client isolation, least-privilege roles, service-account controls, credential management, security review, and documented offboarding.

### Platform dependency

**Risk:** Google, Etsy, Printful, or another platform may change pricing, APIs, or access.  
**Mitigation:** Modular integrations, exportable client records, model abstraction where practical, monitoring, and explicit dependency documentation.

### Custom-service trap

**Risk:** Every client may require too much unique implementation to scale.  
**Mitigation:** Track reusable versus custom work and restrict the initial customer segment and offer.

### Underpricing and support burden

**Risk:** Low retainers may not cover maintenance.  
**Mitigation:** Measure support minutes, define service levels, charge separately for expansion work, and revisit pricing after pilots.

### Overclaiming AI visibility or IP protection

**Risk:** Structured data cannot guarantee recommendation by external AI systems, and access controls cannot guarantee that an artist’s public style will never be imitated.  
**Mitigation:** Promise discoverability practices, source-file controls, provenance, and monitoring—not guaranteed rankings, citations, appraisal gains, or elimination of style theft.

### Founder capacity

**Risk:** Product development, sales, support, and family responsibilities may exceed one founder’s sustainable capacity.  
**Mitigation:** Narrow scope, staged pilots, explicit work limits, documentation, automation only after validation, and contractor planning when economics support it.
