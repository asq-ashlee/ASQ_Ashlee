# Arts of August — Photography and Master File SOP

Status: Active SOP — Version 1.0

## Purpose

This SOP defines the standard photography and master-file process for all Arts of August original paintings and prints. The goal is color-accurate, consistent, professional images that can be used across Etsy listings, print fulfillment, galleries, and marketing channels.

Every artwork goes through the same three-shot sequence at the photo station. Consistency across the collection matters more than perfection in any single session.

Large image assets live outside the repo. Reference them intentionally in artwork records and workflows instead of storing or scanning full asset folders as routine AI context.

## Photo Station Setup

Use these fixed settings for every session. Check each item before shooting.

* White sweep background
* White light box in place
* Lights angled toward side walls, not directly at the subject
* No exposure adjustment; keep settings neutral
* AE/AF Lock enabled
* 2x zoom
* Top-down shooting position
* White balance set; check with a sheet of white paper before starting

### Setup Note

Check white balance at the start of every session, not just the first one. Lighting conditions shift, and a small white-balance drift is enough to throw off color accuracy across an entire batch.

## The Three-Shot Sequence

Every painting gets three shots. Shoot them in order for each artwork before moving to the next artwork.

| Shot Type | Purpose | What It Sells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Archive | Color-accurate record | Confidence |
| 2 — Detail | Brushwork, texture, or gold leaf | Craftsmanship |
| 3 — Lifestyle / Scale | Painting in context or to scale | Imagination |

## Shot 1 — Archive

The archive shot is the baseline, color-accurate record of the painting. Use this shot for print fulfillment, Etsy listings, and any context where true color matters. This is the reference image for the piece.

* Shoot top-down as a flat lay against the white sweep.
* Use consistent framing so the painting fills the frame with even margins.
* Do not use a creative angle, shadow, or props.
* Treat color accuracy as the only goal.

### Why It Works

The archive shot sells confidence. A buyer who can see the true colors and proportions of a piece does not have to guess, and buyers who do not have to guess are buyers who add to cart.

## Shot 2 — Detail

The detail shot is a close-up that captures what makes the painting worth looking at twice: brushwork, impasto texture, gold leaf, layering, or whatever is most interesting about the specific piece.

* Move in close and focus on one area of the painting.
* Choose the detail that best shows Kaleigh's hand in the work.
* Use a slight angle when it helps catch texture in the light.
* Prefer one strong detail shot over several weak ones.

### Why It Works

The detail shot sells craftsmanship. It shows that this is not a print-on-demand product. It is an original object made by a person with skill, which places it in a different category entirely.

## Shot 3 — Lifestyle / Scale

The lifestyle or scale shot shows the painting in a real-world context: on an easel, leaned against a wall, on a shelf, or held in hand. This shot answers the question every buyer has but does not always ask: "What will this look like in my space?"

* Use an easel, wall, shelf, or in-hand setting based on what fits the piece.
* Keep the background clean and neutral: context without clutter.
* Use natural or warm light when helpful. This shot is about feeling, not color accuracy.
* Include a hand or familiar object when it helps communicate scale.

### Why It Works

The lifestyle shot sells imagination. When a buyer can picture a painting in their home, they stop evaluating it and start wanting it. This is the shot that closes the sale.

## Post-Processing

Keep editing minimal and consistent. The goal is a clean, accurate image, not a heavily edited one.

* Crop to remove sweep edges or table edges.
* Rotate to correct orientation if needed.
* Check color against the physical painting before exporting.
* Export a full-resolution master file for archive and print use.
* Export a web-optimized version for Etsy and social use.
* Store large image exports outside the repo and add intentional references where the artwork intake workflow requires them.

## Master File Approval Checklist

Before an artwork image is treated as an approved master file, confirm:

* [ ] The archive shot is top-down, evenly framed, and free of props or distracting shadows.
* [ ] The artwork color has been checked against the physical piece.
* [ ] The image is correctly oriented and cropped.
* [ ] The full-resolution master export has been created.
* [ ] The web-optimized export has been created.
* [ ] The detail shot clearly shows craftsmanship, texture, or a distinctive feature.
* [ ] The lifestyle or scale shot gives useful context without clutter.
* [ ] The external asset location has been recorded intentionally for the artwork intake workflow.
* [ ] The artwork is ready to be logged in the intake tracker.

## Session Workflow

Use this order of operations for each photography session.

1. Set up the station and check all fixed settings.
2. Verify white balance with white paper.
3. Shoot all three shots for artwork #1 before moving to artwork #2.
4. Review the shots on screen before moving on; reshoot if color or framing is off.
5. Apply minimal post-processing and compare color against the physical artwork.
6. Export the full-resolution master file and web-optimized version.
7. Confirm the Master File Approval Checklist.
8. Transfer files to the approved external destination and name them according to the active artwork-intake naming convention once finalized.
9. Log the completed artwork in the intake tracker with intentional asset references.

## Design Notes

This SOP is based on Kaleigh's notes following the first photo-station session. The three-shot framework and the language around what each shot sells come directly from her observations and should remain part of the operating system.

Future improvements should come through versioned updates after the workflow is tested across the collection. The current station and three-shot sequence are ready to use.
